\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The audit question for human--AI complementarity is not whether the human carries information the model lacks but whether that information would change the reward-relevant action.
We named the missing estimand boundary regret and proved that it equals the value of consulting the human in any finite-action Bayesian decision problem; that quantity is strictly positive precisely when some reachable posterior crosses a decision facet.
The plug-in score this identity yields behaves as the bridge predicts: it allocates scarce review where decision-relevant mass is reachable and converges on reward-aware margin where it is not.
No residual-expertise audit produces either behavior.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Boundary regret characterizes the reward-local decision value latent in the joint distribution of $(x, h, y)$.
Whether a deployed workflow elicits and uses that value depends on the elicitation interface and on behavioral frictions \citep{Vaccaro2024,BucinaEtAl2021} the estimand is not designed to model.
The deployed score is a plug-in estimate of an exact identity; systematic error in either the model belief or human-augmented posterior can change its magnitude and false-positive rate when it moves an estimate across a reward facet.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
Routing scarce human review to cases that change the reward-relevant action raises operational efficiency in high-stakes settings such as clinical reading and triage.
But the same machinery, fed a misspecified or contested reward, will under-allocate review for populations whose decision facets it does not reach.
Any operational use of $\BRhat$ should pair the score with reward elicitation and a subgroup audit of which populations it removes human review from.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Boundary regret does not single out the human as the information source.
The same identity prices any candidate channel (a second-opinion reader, an additional diagnostic test, a more expensive feature) by what it would change about the reward-relevant action.
A reward-faithful training signal for learning-to-defer follows: replace accuracy-based deferral labels with the regret of not consulting, and the construction extends to multi-expert and sequential-elicitation policies.
A prospective deployment routing review on $\BRhat$ under operational rewards is the direct test of how much latent decision value a real workflow can extract.
Complementarity is reward-relative. A signal can be informative under any proper scoring rule while leaving the model-only action optimal. The audit that ignores the boundary measures the wrong thing.
