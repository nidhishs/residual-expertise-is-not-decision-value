\section{Boundary Regret}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Decision Setup}
We work in finite-action Bayesian decision problems \citep{DeGroot1970,Howard1966}.
An instance with features $x \in \mathcal{X}$ has unknown state $y \in \mathcal{Y} = \{1,\ldots,K\}$.
The decision-maker chooses an action $a$ from a finite set $\mathcal{A}$, $|\mathcal{A}| \geq 2$, and receives reward $r_a(y)$ specified by $R \in \R^{|\mathcal{A}| \times K}$.
A \emph{belief} $b \in \Delta^{K-1}$ is a probability vector over states; write $b_x = P(Y \mid x)$ for the model belief at instance $x$.
At any belief, the terminal value is the largest expected reward available from the action set.
The model-only action $a_x$ attains this value under $b_x$.
Each action has a decision region: the beliefs at which that action is optimal.
Formally,
\begin{equation}
    V(b) \;=\; \max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} r_a \cdot b,
    \qquad
    a_x \in \argmax_{a \in \mathcal{A}} r_a \cdot b_x,
    \qquad
    \mathcal{R}_a
    \;=\;
    \{b : r_a \cdot b \geq r_{a'} \cdot b\;\forall a' \in \mathcal{A}\}.
\label{eq:decision-setup}
\end{equation}
Each $\mathcal{R}_a$ is a convex polytope, and the relative boundary $\mathcal{R}_a \cap \mathcal{R}_{a'}$ is a \emph{decision facet}.
The regret of action $a$ at belief $b$ is $\operatorname{regret}(a, b) = V(b) - r_a \cdot b \geq 0$, with equality iff $a$ is optimal.

\subsection{Boundary-Regret Estimand}
After observing the human signal $h$, the model belief $b_x$ updates to $b_{x,h}$.
That update has decision value only if it crosses a decision facet, making some rival action better than $a_x$.

\begin{definition}[Boundary Regret]\label{def:br-instance}
For an instance $x$ with model-only action $a_x$ and human signal $h$, the \emph{instance-level boundary regret} is the regret of retaining $a_x$ after the belief updates from $b_x$ to $b_{x,h}$.
The \emph{ex ante boundary regret} is the expected decision value of the human signal at instance $x$, before $h$ is observed.
Formally,
\begin{equation}
    \BR(x, h) \;=\; V(b_{x,h}) \;-\; r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h},
    \qquad
    \BR(x) \;=\; \E_{h \mid x}[\BR(x, h)].
\label{eq:boundary-regret}
\end{equation}
We call $\BR(x,h)$ the \emph{oracle} boundary regret; $\BRhat$ denotes its plug-in estimate (\S\ref{par:estimation}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Decision-Value Identity}
The value of observing the human signal is exactly expected boundary regret.
This rewrites value of information as the loss from keeping the model-only action after the posterior update.

\begin{theorem}[VoI as Expected Boundary Regret]\label{thm:voi-br}
In any finite-action Bayesian decision problem with $a_x$ optimal at $b_x$, where $b_{x,h}$ is the Bayesian posterior update of $b_x$ after observing $h$, the value of observing the human signal equals expected boundary regret:
\begin{equation}
    \VoI(H \mid b_x)
    \;:=\; \E_{h \mid x}\bigl[V(b_{x,h})\bigr] - V(b_x)
    \;=\; \E_{h \mid x}\bigl[\BR(x, h)\bigr]
    \;=\; \BR(x).
\label{eq:voi-br}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\vspace{-1.25em}
\begin{proof}
Use the Bayesian martingale identity: the posterior averages back to the prior.
Therefore the expected reward of any fixed action is unchanged after averaging over the posterior update:
\[
    \E_{h \mid x}[b_{x,h}] = b_x,
    \qquad
    \E_{h \mid x}[r_a \cdot b_{x,h}] = r_a \cdot b_x
    \quad\text{for any fixed action } a.
\]
The left-hand side is the definition of value of information.
Then use the identity above and the optimality of $a_x$ at $b_x$.
Subtract the fixed-action value from the posterior value:
\[
    \E_{h \mid x}[V(b_{x,h})] - V(b_x)
    =
    \E_{h \mid x}[V(b_{x,h})] - \E_{h \mid x}[r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h}]
    =
    \E_{h \mid x}[V(b_{x,h}) - r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h}].
    \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Boundary Crossing}
Boundary regret is positive exactly when at least one reachable human-updated posterior leaves the incumbent decision region.
If every reachable posterior stays in $\mathcal{R}_{a_x}$, the human signal may improve prediction, but the model-only action remains optimal.

\begin{theorem}[Facet Activation Bridge]\label{thm:facet-bridge}
For a coherent Bayesian belief/update pair $(b_x,b_{x,h})$, there is positive ex ante boundary regret if and only if some reachable posterior strictly prefers a rival action to the model-only action.
Equivalently, boundary regret is zero if and only if every reachable posterior remains in the model-only action's decision region:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \BR(x) > 0
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \exists h,\, a' \neq a_x
        \text{ with } P(h \mid x) > 0
        \text{ and }
        r_{a'} \cdot b_{x,h} > r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h},\\
    \BR(x) = 0
    &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        b_{x,h} \in \mathcal{R}_{a_x}
        \text{ for every } h \text{ with } P(h \mid x) > 0.
    \end{aligned}
\label{eq:facet-activation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\vspace{-1.25em}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:voi-br}, ex ante boundary regret is the expectation of the non-negative instance-level regret.
Thus $\BR(x) > 0$ iff some reachable $h$ has positive instance-level boundary regret.
By definition, this means $V(b_{x,h}) > r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h}$, which holds iff some action $a'$ achieves $r_{a'} \cdot b_{x,h} > r_{a_x} \cdot b_{x,h}$.
The converse follows by contraposition: if $a_x$ is optimal at every reachable posterior, then $\BR(x,h) = 0$ for all $h$, hence $\BR(x) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No Crossing, No Decision Value]\label{cor:zero-dv}
If every reachable human-updated posterior $b_{x,h}$ lies in $\mathcal{R}_{a_x}$, then $\VoI(H \mid b_x) = 0$, regardless of how much $H$ improves predictions as measured by log-loss, calibration, or any proper scoring rule.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Deployment and Estimation}
\paragraph{Potential vs.\ Realized Value.}
$\BR(x)$ is an information-theoretic ceiling on the per-instance decision value of human review.
To realize that value, a system still needs correct elicitation, calibrated posterior updating, and operator compliance \citep{Vaccaro2024,BucinaEtAl2021}.
Boundary regret isolates the reward-local ceiling for any deferral policy or interface design.

\paragraph{Estimation.}\label{par:estimation}
The identities above are exact for ideal Bayesian beliefs: $b_x$ and $b_{x,h}$ are a coherent prior/posterior update pair.
Empirically, we replace these objects with estimates.
The estimated model belief determines $a_x$; a cross-fitted human-augmented model estimates the updated belief $\bpost_{x,h} \approx P(Y \mid x,h)$.

Thus $\BRhat$ is a plug-in estimate of boundary regret, not a new theoretical identity.
For ex ante review allocation, we also estimate $P(h \mid x)$ before human review and compute
\begin{equation}
    \widehat\E_{h \mid x}\!\left[V(\bpost_{x,h}) - r_{a_x} \cdot \bpost_{x,h}\right].
\label{eq:ex-ante-brhat}
\end{equation}
Calibration affects how accurately this plug-in score recovers true boundary regret; Appendix~\ref{app:exp1-miscalibration} reports a targeted binary stress test of systematic belief error.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} also details how this quantity is estimated for each experiment.
